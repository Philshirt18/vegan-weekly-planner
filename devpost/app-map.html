Vegan Weekly Planner – App Map

A short take-home guide to how the app is built and where to change things. It is a snapshot of commit de449b6 and can drift as the code changes. Everything here works offline; no scripts are needed.

What the app does, in five pieces

A weekly meal planner for vegan families. You add your family, choose dishes that are already balanced for nutrients, and the app spreads them over the week, warns when two calcium-rich dishes fall on one day, and builds a shopping list scaled to your family. Each week has its own plan and list.

  1. Screens – src/pages/ (welcome, sign-in, family, dishes, detail, days, week plan, shopping).
  2. The 17 dishes – src/data/dishes.js and the nutrition per ingredient in src/data/ingredients.js.
  3. Rules and calculations – src/lib/ (nutrition guideline values, dish nutrition, week plan, shopping list, recipe amounts, weeks).
  4. Saving – src/lib/AppState.jsx holds the state; src/lib/firebase.js is the only file that talks to Firebase (sign-in and the database).
  5. Look – src/styles/theme.css (colours, font, round shapes).

A route through the code

A reference route through one real decision. Search for the names in your editor.

  1. Where the decision lives: src/lib/dishNutrients.js, search CALCIUM_RICH_MG and classify. A dish is calcium-rich from 300 mg calcium, and iron-rich only if it is not calcium-rich and has at least 5 mg iron. So a dish is never both.
  2. Where it is applied: src/lib/planner.js, search calciumWarnings. It checks every day of the plan and returns the days where lunch and dinner are both calcium-rich.
  3. Where you see it: src/pages/WeekPlan.jsx, search Keep it anyway. The note and its button are shown for each day in that list.

The same rule is checked by tests: tests/dishes.test.js ("never labels a dish calcium-rich and iron-rich at the same time") and tests/planner.test.js ("warns only when both meals of a day are calcium-rich"). Run them with npm test.

To change this, start here

Change the wording of a message or button

Search for the text in src/pages/, for example Keep it anyway in WeekPlan.jsx. Nothing else depends on it.

Change the calcium/iron rule

src/lib/dishNutrients.js: CALCIUM_RICH_MG and IRON_RICH_MG. Then run npm test: the dish tests will tell you if the labels no longer fit (for example if there are too few or too many calcium-rich dishes for the planner to avoid same-day pairs).

Change a recipe or add a dish

src/data/dishes.js (ingredients and steps). A new ingredient also needs an entry in src/data/ingredients.js. The picture goes to public/images/<dish-id>.jpg; scripts/convert-images.sh prepares it. The nutrition is computed from the ingredients, so do not type it in.

Change how portions are counted

src/lib/nutrition.js: portionFactor (an adult counts 1, a child up to 12 counts 0.5). It scales the shopping list.

Change how far ahead you can plan

src/lib/weeks.js: MIN_OFFSET and MAX_OFFSET.

Change colours or the font

The variables at the top of src/styles/theme.css.

Change who may read or write the data

firestore.rules (currently: a signed-in person may only read and write their own entry). Paste changed rules into the Firebase console.

A practice to reuse

Turn a rule into something you can check, then let a check challenge it.

The first version of the rule was "calcium and iron never in the same dish". Once the nutrition of each dish was computed from its ingredients (computeNutrients in src/lib/dishNutrients.js), a check showed that 8 to 9 of the 17 dishes broke the rule, because tofu, beans, kale and sesame contain both minerals. The rule was then changed on purpose to a measurable version (a 300 mg threshold in classify), written down in devpost/prd.md under Product Decisions, and locked in by the tests above.

On another project: write a requirement so that a test or a number can show whether it holds, and when a check disagrees with the requirement, decide whether the requirement or the data should change and record the reason.
